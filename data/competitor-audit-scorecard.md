# Competitor Review Scorecard — AI-first English Learning

Use this with two reviewers: one learner with functional/basic English and one professionally fluent English user. They should independently test the same journey before discussing it together.

This does **not** score who has the best marketing. It tests whether the product earns repeat use, whether AI replaces enough teacher time, and whether its commercial design can work at the local price point.

## Test protocol

- Devices: one budget Android and one mid-range Android; record model, Android version, network, city/state and device language.
- Accounts: new phone/email accounts. Do not use a staff referral or a discount link.
- Plans: document every free limit, trial, price, payment method, auto-renewal disclosure, refund rule and human upsell shown.
- Timing: day 0 onboarding; days 1–7 daily use; days 14 and 30 retention/re-engagement checks. Capture screenshots/video with dates.
- Scenarios: job interview, customer call, workplace update, parent–teacher interaction, travel/service encounter, and one free conversation chosen by the learner.
- Ethics: use only normal consumer access. Do not bypass paywalls, create fake reviews, scrape private data or misrepresent identity to support.

## Reviewer rubric

Score every item 1–5 and leave evidence: exact screen, timestamp, quote, or task outcome.

| Area | What the basic-English reviewer tests | What the fluent reviewer tests | Evidence to save |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery & promise | Is the ad/store promise understandable in my language? | Is the claim specific and believable? | Ad/Play Store URL, landing-page screenshot |
| Onboarding | Can I state my goal and language without embarrassment? | Does it diagnose level/goal rather than use a generic tour? | Time to first speaking task; language options |
| First success | Did I speak in the first 2 minutes? Did feedback make sense? | Is feedback accurate, prioritised and actionable? | First task recording and feedback |
| Localisation | Do examples, UI, translations, accents and payment feel local? | Are code-mixed speech and regional accents handled appropriately? | Misrecognitions / bad translations |
| Curriculum | Can I see a route from today to job/interview outcome? | Is progression valid or only gamified repetition? | Lesson map; stated level standard |
| AI conversation | Does it feel safe enough to try? | Does the AI adapt, remember context, avoid hallucinating corrections? | 3 transcripts across scenarios |
| Feedback quality | Do I know one thing to fix next? | Does it distinguish grammar, clarity, fluency, pronunciation and pragmatics? | Before/after task score |
| Motivation | Is streak/reward useful rather than pressure? | Does it create intrinsic outcome motivation? | Reward/leaderboard rules |
| Habit design | What makes me come back tomorrow? | Are reminders timed/personalised and easy to control? | All notifications and re-entry path |
| Failure / churn | What makes me stop: shame, difficulty, boredom, paywall, accuracy, ads? | Is there a recovery path after a missed day or a poor session? | Drop-off moment and reason |
| Social/human layer | Would I want peer practice or a human? Why? | Does human help solve a real escalation rather than duplicate AI? | Trigger, price and booked experience |
| Trust & safety | Are privacy, recordings and deletion choices clear? | Are consent, payment and support terms credible? | Privacy/support screens |
| Pricing | Is price feasible and is value clear before checkout? | Is annual discount/renewal framing ethical and economics plausible? | All price screens, currency and plans |
| Support & recovery | Can I get help without frustration? | Does support resolve technical/payment issues quickly? | Support channel, response time |
| Outcome proof | Would I pay because it can change a job/service outcome? | Is assessment tied to observable workplace performance? | Certificate/report/call simulation |

## Metrics the product team should calculate

Do not use competitor download count as a proxy for this.

- Time to first completed speaking interaction
- Activation: a learner completes an outcome-relevant activity in first session
- Day 1, 7, 30 and 90 retained active learners
- Speaking minutes per retained learner; feedback acceptance/correction rate
- Trial start → paid conversion; monthly/annual mix; refund rate
- First paid renewal; annual renewal; cohort revenue after payment fees
- AI inference + storage cost per active learner
- Human escalation rate, cost, satisfaction and incremental retention
- Fully-loaded CAC, CAC payback, gross margin and contribution margin by channel
- B2B: manager adoption, learner activation, course completion, assessment gain, contract renewal/expansion

## Decision gates

1. **AI earns the core session:** the learner can practise, understand feedback and see progress without a teacher.
2. **Human is an escalation:** mentor/peer support is reserved for confidence, accountability or edge cases where it increases retention/outcomes enough to pay for itself.
3. **Outcome is visible:** the app ties practice to an interview, customer interaction, promotion, BPO quality score or other concrete milestone.
4. **Distribution pays for itself:** a college, employer, training centre or channel partner lowers CAC versus generic consumer ads.
5. **Price works after variable costs:** use observed local price, payment fee, AI cost, support and human cost—not only subscription revenue.

## CEO readout format (one slide per app)

- Who it serves and the promise
- The first 10 minutes: what works / where it fails
- Retention loop: trigger → session → reward → re-entry
- Monetisation: free limit, paywall, price, renewal, human upsell
- AI economics hypothesis: what AI replaces, what still requires people
- Best idea to adopt; dangerous idea to avoid
- One experiment your company should run in 2 weeks
- Confidence: observed / inferred / unknown

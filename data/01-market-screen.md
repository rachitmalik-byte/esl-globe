# ESL market screening - 30-country summary

**Scope:** AI-first English-speaking learning with optional teacher escalation. **As of:** 2026-10-27.

This is a decision screen, not a market-size forecast. Scores are analyst assessments on a 1-10 scale. They are deliberately separated from observed source data. High English need or a low proficiency score does not establish that learners will pay for a subscription.

## Read this table correctly

- **Outcome demand** means a credible job, BPO, export, tourism, study or mobility reason to improve spoken English.
- **Paid conversion** is the practical likelihood of a viable payer at the intended price. It is not app download volume.
- **Digital delivery** covers practical ability to distribute/use an app. **Distribution/B2B** covers prospects for employer, college, training-centre or partner acquisition.
- **Whitespace** is differentiated room, not absence of competitors. **Operating feasibility** includes localisation, sales cycle, payments, regulation and market execution.
- **Evidence confidence** says how much direct evidence backs the assessment. Do not use low-confidence scores as facts.

## Current strategic read

1. **India:** build and prove the model with one regional-language, job-linked cohort and a B2B/B2B2C payer. Consumer download scale is not enough.
2. **Indonesia:** closest next low-price, mobile-first test; require Bahasa onboarding and partner distribution.
3. **Mexico and Colombia:** BPO/nearshoring and employer pathways make them better for targeted B2B than broad consumer subscriptions. Colombia is a mid-to-strong niche, not a blanket country winner.
4. **Saudi Arabia / UAE:** purchasing power is strong, but the route is enterprise/procurement, Arabic localisation and longer sales cycles, not low-price B2C.
5. **Vietnam, Thailand, Morocco, Egypt and Turkey:** real role-specific demand, but each needs local-language fit, a channel partner, and proof that CAC remains below value.
6. **Philippines and Malaysia:** useful benchmarks for BPO/professional English. Their relatively high proficiency and mature supply reduce the need for basic remedial English.
